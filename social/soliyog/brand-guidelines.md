# Soliyog — Brand Voice & Social Strategy v1.0

_Last updated: 2026-08-29_

## Quick Reference

- **What Soliyog is:** A career platform pairing real IT & finance job openings with guides that explain what recruiters actually test.
- **Primary goal of social:** Drive qualified traffic to guides and listings → signups.
- **Audience:** Indian students & recent grads (final year to ~2 yrs experience) targeting entry-level IT (software, data) and finance (equity research, CA-track, portfolio/analyst) roles.
- **Voice in three words:** Straight-talking, practical, on their side.
- **The wedge:** Everyone else sells hype. Soliyog sells realism.

---

## 1. Positioning

> **Soliyog is the career platform for Indian freshers who want a realistic path into IT and finance jobs — pairing real openings with guides that explain what recruiters actually test.**

**Positioning statement**
Soliyog is the career-prep platform for Indian freshers who want to break into IT and finance without a top-tier college or inflated promises, because it pairs honest role breakdowns with live job listings and interview guidance grounded in what recruiters actually screen for.

**Value proposition**
For final-year students and early-career professionals who don't know what a role really involves or how to get in, Soliyog is a career platform that pairs 210+ real openings with step-by-step guides. Unlike influencers selling "₹50 LPA in 3 months," Soliyog is honest about the effort, the timeline, and the parts nobody mentions (referrals, screening filters, week-one reality).

**Competitors in the audience's feed**
- "Crack the interview" influencers with inflated salary claims
- Campus-hiring content (Unstop, Naukri Campus)
- LinkedIn hustle culture / motivational posting
- Generic upskilling course ads

Soliyog wins by being the calm, honest voice in that feed.

## 2. Messaging Framework

**Mission**
We give Indian freshers a realistic path into IT and finance careers by pairing real job openings with guides on what recruiters test, so they can prepare for the job that actually exists — not the one in the brochure.

**Primary message**
Know what the job really is. Know what they test. Then apply.

**Supporting messages**

| Message | Audience need | Proof point |
|---|---|---|
| "See what the role actually involves before you commit." | Fear of picking the wrong track | 8 role guides covering day-to-day work |
| "Prep for what recruiters screen for, not generic tips." | Wasted prep effort | Guides map to real interview rounds |
| "Real openings, updated — not a stale board." | Applying into the void | 210+ current listings across IT & finance |
| "You can break in without a top-tier college." | Imposter feeling / gatekeeping | Realistic ~3-month prep framing, entry paths |
| "The referral matters more than the resume — here's how." | Nobody explains the hidden game | Job-search tactics content |

**Elevator pitch (30s)**
Most freshers prep for an imaginary version of the job. Soliyog pairs 210+ real IT and finance openings with guides that break down what each role actually involves day-to-day and what recruiters screen for in each round — so your prep maps to the job that exists. No hype, no inflated salary promises, just a realistic path in.

## 3. Voice

### Personality traits

| Trait | Means | Sounds like | Never sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Straight-talking** | Plain facts, no jargon, no hype | "Equity research analysts spend week one building models in Excel. Here's what that actually means." | "Unlock your DREAM career today! 🚀" |
| **Practical** | Every post gives a usable next step | "3 SQL patterns that show up in data analyst interviews — drill these this week." | Vague motivation with no action |
| **On their side** | Advocate for the fresher; name the hard parts out loud | "Nobody tells you the referral matters more than the resume. So let's talk referrals." | Talking down, or "just work harder" |
| **Grounded optimism** | Encouraging, but honest about the effort | "You can break in without a top-tier college. It takes ~3 months of focused prep, not luck." | Toxic positivity, or doom-posting |

### Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Educational post | Calm, teacherly | "Here's how a data analyst interview is usually structured…" |
| Job-opening spotlight | Direct, useful | "5 equity research analyst roles open this week. Fresher-friendly. Links below." |
| Reply to a discouraged comment | Warm, specific, no platitudes | "Rejection after the final round usually means it was close. Ask for feedback, then apply to 3 similar roles this week." |
| Rejection / hard-truth topic | Honest but constructive | "Most freshers get filtered at the resume screen, not the interview. Fixable. Here's what the screen looks for." |

### Prohibited

- Inflated or specific salary promises ("₹50 LPA guaranteed") — kills the realism wedge
- "Dream job / dream career" as a hook — overused, vague
- Fake urgency ("Last chance!", "Only today!")
- Guru / "secret" framing ("the ONE trick recruiters don't want you to know")
- Talking down to freshers ("obviously", "everyone knows")
- Excessive emojis and rocket ships; one purposeful emoji max per post
- Em-dashes as a stylistic tic; hedging filler ("it's worth noting that", "in today's competitive landscape")

### Sample rewrites

**Before:** "🚀 Want to land your DREAM job in tech?? Our guides will UNLOCK your potential! Link in bio 👇✨"
**After:** "Not sure what a data analyst actually does all day? We wrote a guide that walks through a real week in the role — plus the 12 openings hiring freshers right now. Link in bio."

**Before:** "Motivation Monday: believe in yourself and success will follow! You got this! 💪"
**After:** "Monday reset: pick 3 roles you'll apply to this week and 1 skill you'll drill. Small and specific beats motivated and vague. (Guides for the drilling part in bio.)"

## 6. Traffic Mechanics

- **One CTA per post**, pointing to a *specific* guide or listing page — never a bare "visit soliyog.com".
  - Good: "Full equity research interview guide → soliyog.com/guides/equity-research-analyst"
  - Bad: "Check out our website for more!"
- **80/20 rule:** teach 80% in the post itself; the link is for the deeper 20% (full guide, the actual listings, the checklist).
- **Openings posts** always link to the filtered listing page for that role/city, not the homepage.
- **Bio link** per platform → a simple landing page (guides + "browse openings"), not the raw homepage.
- **First comment** on LinkedIn/IG carries the link (keeps the post in-feed while still routing traffic).
- Track which pillar and which guide drives signups; shift the mix toward what converts after ~4 weeks.

## 7. Do / Don't Cheat Sheet

**Do**
- Lead with the specific, slightly uncomfortable truth ("Most freshers get filtered before a human reads the resume.")
- Name real roles, real rounds, real cities
- Give one action per post
- Reply to every substantive comment in the first 2 hours
- Use numbers from the platform (210+ openings, 8 guides) as proof, sparingly

**Don't**
- Promise outcomes or salaries
- Post motivation without a mechanism
- Bury the value below a link
- Use more than one emoji, or any rocket ship
- Sound like a course ad
